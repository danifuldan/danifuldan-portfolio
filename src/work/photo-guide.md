## The workflow and where it broke

People across teams would ask for things that weren't always clear or aligned with the product and how it actually worked. They were using terms based on the customer or installer or whoever they'd last talked to, and there was no common point of reference, no source of truth.

## The insight

People lacked a clear concept of the equipment, how it looked, and what it was for. So I decided to build one.

## The build

Most were familiar with the primary components (the main cameras, for instance). But many of the components were smaller, things that were simply included in a package, for example. My thought was, the best way to help people get a mental model of the equipment was photos and descriptions of purpose. 

So we needed to take some photos. One of my colleagues was formerly a photographer at a professional studio, so I recruited him to the project. We planned for time and equipment and held a photoshoot in the office for every item, one after another.

Then it was time to put the raw files together. I edited the photos in Photoshop to isolate the subjects and put them into InDesign pages, with short descriptions of each individual item and groups of equipment / kits / etc. 

I distributed this PDF to everyone who would accept it; it was hosted on our company intranet as well as emailed to every manager I could think might care. 

## What changed

I can't say this moved any specific metrics, but between this and the form I started seeing people refer to the same equipment by the same term. And when people seemed unclear in conversation, I was able to use the individual photos to clarify much, much faster than before. 

I've been told that the document is still a support resource in use; my colleague, who graduated to my team from the support team, said they used to use it all the time and called it the "parts Bible". 

Elements of it also went into a few different internal documents, such as a page geared towards new customers (showing what equipment to expect, etc.). My boss continues to ask for reference photos on occasion, so the project as a whole continues to pay dividends. 

## Reflection

If I did it again I would build a series of knowledge base entries, not a PDF. However, offers to convert it to the internal KB at the time were rebuffed (not my lane, to be fair). The content would carry over, but PDF is simply not an easily maintained format. 

This project was one of my most enjoyable experiences at this job; planning, executing, and delivering something outside of typical processes that provides ongoing value was a delight.
